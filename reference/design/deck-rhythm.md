# Deck rhythm

A deck reads as designed when its slides change shape with the argument, not when every slide is different.

- **Vary the shape.** Never the same variant three times in a row. After two or three dense slides (a chart, a table,
  a scorecard), give the room a moment: a statement, a giant number, a quote.
- **Moments are rare.** Full-bleed slides are for the cover, part openers, one or two statements and the closing. On a
  company template keep them to about one slide in five, and check the style guide: some forbid covering the logo,
  and then every slide should be a default or a title-only variant.
- **Repeat what should feel the same.** Every part opener uses the same section variant; an agenda / rail between
  parts shows where the talk stands.
- **Keep color meanings.** An option keeps its data color from the options slide to the last chart.
- **Live or read-ahead.** A live talk has one idea per slide and more moments, with the detail in the speaker notes. A
  read-ahead uses denser title-only variants and fewer moments; every caveat and source sits on the slide.
- **The closing asks.** End on what to do next, never on "Questions?".
