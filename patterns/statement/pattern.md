# Pattern: statement

**Answers:** What is the one sentence to remember?

## Content

```json
{"kicker": "What decides it", "proof": [{"label": "What was measured", "value": "4.4% to 10.7%"}], "source": "Where the proof comes from."}
```

The statement is the headline: one sentence, said so plainly it can be repeated. `emphasis` on the storyboard slide
(`["never"]`) names the headline words the variant colors. Up to three proof points, each a label and a value.

## Variants

- **`with-proof`** (full-bleed): the sentence at display size, its emphasis words in the signal color, a strip of
  proof under a hairline.
- **`centered`** (full-bleed): the sentence alone, centred at display size with its emphasis words in the signal color, a kicker over it and its source under it. The quietest moment a deck has.
- **`color-field`** (full-bleed): the whole slide in the signal color, the sentence in the ground color and its emphasis words in the ink color. Once per deck at most.

## What suits it

The turn of the argument, the finding the deck exists for, or a rule the audience should leave with. One or two
per deck: a statement slide works because the others don't look like it.

## Density

- **Live:** the sentence alone can work; proof makes it credible.
- **Read-ahead:** always the proof and its source.

## Common failures

- A sentence over about 20 words: it stops being a statement.
- Emphasis on more than one or two words.
- Proof values with no claim behind them (`convey check` refuses them).
