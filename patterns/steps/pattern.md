# Pattern: steps

**Answers:** What do I do, in what order?

## Content

```json
{"steps": [{"label": "Step one", "note": "What to do, in one line."}, {"label": "Step two", "note": "..."}]}
```

The same slots as diagram's steps: a short label and a one-line note each. On `row`, a step may also carry a `tag`
(two or three words, such as "You sign off") at the foot of its panel, to mark the steps the headline singles out. Compose numbers the discs; a step's
number is never a claim.

## Variants

- **`two-rows`** (title-only): five to eight steps in two rows of four, each disc threaded to the next.
- **`row`** (title-only): three to five steps as raised panels in one row, numbered on signal discs, a pointer between each. Bigger type, for fewer steps or a live room. `two-rows` and `vertical` with a note per step are read-ahead at full length: in a live talk, keep the notes short or move them to the speaker notes.
- **`vertical`** (title-only): three to six steps down the slide, each a numbered disc on a rail with its label and note on one line: reads like a checklist.

## What suits it

A procedure the audience will follow: a checklist, a method, a rollout. When the order matters less than the
parts, use executive-summary; when parts feed each other, use diagram.

## Density

- **Live:** labels; say the notes.
- **Read-ahead:** labels and notes.

## Common failures

- Steps that are really categories.
- Notes longer than two lines at 18 px.
